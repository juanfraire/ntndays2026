About NTN Days 2026

NTN Days 2026 is an event dedicated to Non-Terrestrial Networks, to be held on October 13–14, 2026, at Cnam in Paris. The objective of these days is to bring together academic and industrial communities working on the multiple challenges of non-terrestrial connectivity, spanning from the physical layer to networking and standardization.

Hassan Fawaz · Univity

From Vision to Validation: Univity's 5G NTN Journey slides

Abstract and bio

This keynote will introduce Univity's vision for 5G Non-Terrestrial Networks and present some of the key building blocks developed to turn that vision into a validated system. Through uniSpark, uniShape and the company's digital-twin environment, the presentation will highlight how architecture, emulation and end-to-end testing are used to progressively design, integrate and de-risk satellite-based 5G connectivity.

Hassan Fawaz is a 5G Software Architect at Univity, working on 5G NTN architecture, RAN/Core integration and system validation for satellite broadband networks. He holds a PhD in Telecommunications and previously worked in research on wireless resource management and AI/ML for networks.

09:00 – 09:45
Keynote

François Baccelli · Inria, Télécom Paris, LINCS

Research on Non-Terrestrial Networks: Design Tools, Regulation, and Cooperation

Abstract and bio

This talk presents a research and innovation programme on non-terrestrial networks (NTN) built on three complementary components. The first is a set of advanced analytical tools for the design of efficient, high-performance NTN: recent theoretical results that characterize and predict the statistics of spectral efficiency in large-scale deployments, and that support the design of constellation architectures, routing strategies and critical performance metrics such as latency as a function of the constellation parameters and architecture. Ongoing work extends these methods to terrestrial/non-terrestrial integration, to sensing capabilities, to reconfigurable intelligent surfaces. The second component is interdisciplinary and regulatory: following the 2024 report of the Académie des sciences on large satellite constellations, NTN were selected as one of the three scientific priorities of the S7 process for the 2026 G7 summit, and the resulting joint statement of the G7 academies calls for an international, science-based regulatory framework ensuring the sustainable use of low Earth orbit. The third component is industrial and international cooperation, with partners such as Univity, Greenerwave and ONERA, and with universities in South Korea.

François Baccelli is a research director at Inria Paris and a visiting professor at Télécom Paris, where he co-founded the LINCS joint laboratory. He leads the ERC Advanced project NEMO on network mathematics. His work covers point processes, stochastic geometry and the dynamics of large networks, and he is the co-author of reference books on stochastic geometry and wireless networks. A member of the French Academy of Sciences since 2005, he received the Grand Prix France Télécom in 2002, the ACM SIGMETRICS Achievement Award and the IEEE Rice and Abraham prizes in 2014, and the Blackwell Prize in 2024.

Venue & Location

Hosted at the Conservatoire national des arts et métiers (Cnam) in the heart of Paris — a historic institution of science and technology since 1794.

292 Rue Saint-Martin, 75003 Paris, France

Finding the room

  • Amphithéâtre Jean-Baptiste Say (marked JBS on the map), access Y, lower level.
  • Enter by the main entrance at 292 Rue Saint-Martin, cross the Cour d'honneur, and take the stairs down on the far side of the courtyard.
  • Registration and the welcome coffee start at 08:30 on Tuesday, just outside the amphitheatre.

Getting there

  • Metro Arts-et-Métiers (lines 3 and 11), right at the Cnam, or Réaumur-Sébastopol (lines 3 and 4), 400 m away.
  • Bus 20, 38, 39 and 47 stop on Boulevard de Sébastopol and Rue Réaumur.
  • From the airports RER B to Châtelet-Les Halles, then metro 4 or a 15-minute walk.
